## About Myself

I am a PhD student in the Statistical Physics and Soft Matter Theory group at the Institute of Physics, Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz. My work brings together computational statistical physics, soft matter, and biophysics to understand how molecular interactions give rise to complex behaviour in biological systems.

My doctoral research focuses on the phase behaviour and dynamics of RNA-binding proteins, particularly FUS and TDP-43, and the role of intrinsically disordered protein regions. I study how these proteins form biomolecular condensates through liquid–liquid phase separation, and how their molecular features influence the properties of these assemblies. As part of CRC 1551, my work explores polymer concepts related to post-translational modifications of RNA-binding proteins linked to neurodegeneration.

I use coarse-grained and multiscale molecular dynamics simulations on high-performance computing systems to connect molecular interactions with collective behaviour. I am also interested in developing efficient simulation approaches and using data science and machine learning to extract physical insight from complex simulation data. My earlier research explored diffusion in crowded lipid membranes and the unbinding transition of grafted polymers.

Alongside research, I have taught tutorials in statistical physics and quantum mechanics and contributed to a coarse-graining tutorial. You can read more about my [research]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}) and [publications]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}), or find my academic background in my [CV]({{ '/cv/' | relative_url }}).

I do not love to travel a lot, but I spend most of the time sitting in the office. Apart from that, I love to discuss a variety of things with people and confuse them 🙂. In my spare time, I like to read philosophical ideas and time to implement them in everyday life.
